# Portfolio voice

The site is written by Anand in the first person and speaks plainly. The approved copy in
`docs/sdlc/001-portfolio-v1/intent.md` and in the Figma template (Template - Portfolio,
node 588:2) is the reference. Match it; do not invent a second voice.

## The rules

1. **First person singular.** "I", "my". Never "Anand is", never "we", never "the candidate".
2. **Plain words, short sentences.** One idea per sentence. No jargon a recruiter outside IT
   would not know, unless it is a tool name.
3. **Concrete beats general.** Numbers, tool names, dates and outcomes: "96.2% CSAT",
   "over 150 fixed or created", "since 2023". Never "significant", "various", "numerous".
4. **No hype.** Banned: passionate, leverage, synergy, cutting-edge, world-class, rockstar,
   ninja, guru, seamless, robust, innovative, dynamic, results-driven, proven track record.
   No exclamation marks anywhere on the site.
5. **Honest levels.** The Skills section rule is the whole site's rule: anything only read
   about is not claimed. Do not upgrade "helped" to "led" or "supported" to "managed".
6. **Verb-first bullets.** Experience bullets start with a verb or the metric, are fragments
   not sentences, and end with a full stop: "Managed Windows and macOS fleets with Active
   Directory, SCCM and Jamf Pro MDM."
7. **Headings say something.** Section headings are sentence case and make a claim:
   "What I can do on day one", "Support is where you learn how systems actually fail".
   The small-caps eyebrow above them is the label (EXPERIENCE, SKILLS). Never two labels.
8. **The through-line.** Eleven years finding out why systems fail, now learning to secure
   them. Every section should be able to point back to it. Support experience is framed as
   security-relevant evidence, not as a past to apologize for.
9. **Contact is warm and direct.** "Say hello", "my inbox is open". The close is designed,
   because it is the last thing read.
10. **Quotes are verbatim.** The recommendation from Manoel Galvao is reproduced exactly as
    it appears on LinkedIn, with name and title. Never trim, paraphrase or "improve" it.

## Link text, alt text and metadata

- Link text stands alone: "Read the case study", "Download my résumé (PDF, 120 KB)", never
  "click here" or "Read more". External links say where they go: "LinkedIn", "GitHub".
- Alt text describes the artifact, not the file: "Token table from the Acme design system,
  light theme", not "screenshot".
- Page title: "Anand Francis – IT support to cybersecurity". Meta description: one plain
  sentence under 155 characters in the same voice that mentions the eleven years, the
  cybersecurity degree and the internship.
- Error and empty states say what happened and what to do next, in the same voice.

## Before reporting copy done

Read it aloud as Anand. If a sentence would sound odd said to a hiring manager across a
desk, rewrite it. Then check it against the banned list and the fixed facts above.
